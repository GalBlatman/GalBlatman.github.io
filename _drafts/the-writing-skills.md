Over the past months I have built a set of reusable skills that encode how top management journals evaluate written work. Each one is a structured set of instructions that an AI assistant loads when a task calls for it. There are four:

**Academic writing.** The prose standards of AMJ, AMR, ASQ, SMJ, and Organization Science, applied at any scale, from a sentence to a full draft.

**Manuscript review.** A pre-submission review at the standard of a demanding reviewer: long, comprehensive, and candid rather than polite.

**Theory contribution.** A stress test for whether an idea contains a genuine theoretical contribution at all, before any prose gets polished.

**Minto pyramid.** Answer-first structure for everything that is not a journal manuscript: memos, response letters, job market documents, talks.

The surprise was not that the skills work. It was what building them required. The standards of a field mostly live as tacit judgment, in editors' heads and in the pattern of reviewer letters, and writing them down forced me to make explicit things I had only half known I knew.
