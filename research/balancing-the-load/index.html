Balancing the Load.

Governance Mode as a Response to Institutional Complexity

Gal Blatman and Shon Hiatt · USC Marshall School of Business

Two firms can pursue similar activities yet govern them differently. When institutional demands conflict, the choice to go alone or bring in partners can be more than an operational decision.

The central argument
A firm can stay in a contested activity without bearing the costs of incompatible demands alone.

Shared governance places other organizations alongside the focal firm. We argue that this can buffer the costs of incompatible demands that any one firm would otherwise bear.

The argument at a glance
01
Why share?

Sharing governance can buffer the costs of incompatible demands while the activity continues.

02
Where does sharing increase?

Challenges to firm direction affect the overall mix. Disputes over activities help explain which deals bring in partners.

03
What matters more for a deal?

Where an activity lies in the contested domain matters more than the challenge to the firm's overall direction.

01 / The mechanism

Keep the activity. Change who bears the burden.

When audiences make incompatible demands, going it alone leaves the focal firm standing behind the activity by itself. Bringing in partners offers a buffer while the activity continues.

02 / A real contrast

Similar low-carbon activities. Different starting points.

What a new activity means depends partly on what the firm already operates. Two deals illustrate the contrast.

ENI · 2020

Entered low-carbon generation with a partner.

Eni's operations were then concentrated toward the conventional end of the transition domain. It entered low-carbon generation through a joint venture with HitecVision.

Joint ventureLarger departure from existing operations
TOTALENERGIES · 2022

Acquired a solar developer outright.

TotalEnergies had already moved further toward transition activities. It acquired Core Solar without sharing governance with a partner.

AcquisitionSmaller departure from existing operations

Both deals sit toward the low-carbon end of the contested energy-transition domain. Their different starting points help illustrate why governance choices can differ.

03 / The theory

What is contested changes where firms bring in partners.

Not every institutional dispute concerns the same thing. The paper distinguishes disputes over organizational direction from disputes over how particular activities are pursued.

01 · GOALS

What should the firm pursue?

Challenges to the firm’s overall direction create a broad reason not to stand alone across its activities.

Prediction: more shared governance in the firm’s overall deal mix.
02 · MEANS

How should an activity be pursued?

Activities occupy different positions in a contested domain. A transition activity may be especially incompatible with a firm whose operations remain conventional.

Prediction: where a deal sits, and how it fits the firm, shape partnering.
04 / The evidence

Three findings from the governance choices.

The study examines how firms govern acquisitions, joint ventures and alliances under competing institutional demands.

Finding 01 / Firm-year

More contestation, more partnering.

As contestation of firm direction rose, the predicted acquisition share fell. The companion model found no evidence that firms responded by doing fewer deals overall.

The contribution

Firms facing incompatible demands do not always have to choose between standing alone and walking away. They can bring in partners and keep pursuing the activity.

Institutional complexity can change who governs an activity, not only whether the firm undertakes it. Shared governance offers a way to buffer the costs of incompatible demands while staying involved.

Balancing the Load: Governance Mode as a Response to Institutional Complexity. Public overview of a working paper. The study documents associations with governance choices; it proposes, but does not test, whether shared governance ultimately lowers the costs of incompatible demands. Charts simplify the model estimates; the manuscript contains uncertainty intervals and robustness analyses.
